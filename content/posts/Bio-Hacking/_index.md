The Claim:
A TikTok creator (whose credentials include "doing her own research" and "just asking questions") claims that air fryers contain antimony, which she links to elevated levels in her autistic child's bloodwork. Therefore, air fryers = autism. Because that's totally how science works, right?
The Reality Check:
1. Antimony 101:
First, let's get something straight - antimony is indeed present in air fryers. Specifically, it's in the power cord, along with basically every other high-voltage appliance in your house. You know, like your beloved coffee maker, that microwave you can't live without, and yes, even your regular oven. By this logic, we should all be cooking over campfires while wearing tin foil hats.
Source: https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/toxfaqs/tfacts23.pdf
2. The Power Cord Revelation:
Here's the kicker - antimony in power cords is bound within the material. Unless your child is literally chewing on live electrical cords (in which case, you might have bigger problems), the chance of antimony exposure from an air fryer is about as likely as me winning the lottery while being struck by lightning... twice.
3. The Actually Interesting Science:
Want to hear something that actually matters about air fryers? Let's talk about acrylamide - you know, that compound that forms when you fry foods at high temperatures (the Maillard reaction, for my fellow science nerds). A recent study suggests that air fryers might actually reduce acrylamide formation compared to traditional frying methods.
Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1466856422003186
The study found that air frying can reduce acrylamide formation by 59.2-78.7% compared to deep fat frying. So not only is your air fryer not causing autism, it might actually be making your food slightly healthier. How's that for a plot twist?
4. Environmental Exposure:
If you're genuinely concerned about antimony exposure (which is valid), look at your environment. Antimony occurs naturally and can be found in food, water, and soil. Living near industrial areas, smelters, or waste processing facilities is far more likely to affect antimony levels than your kitchen appliances.
